## §1. Suggested framing and narrative arc

**Position the paper as a pilot, hypothesis-generating analysis.** Not a clinical validation, not a definitive epidemiology study. The cohort is 86 AFI cases — appropriate for generating leads, not for prevalence claims. Make this clear in the abstract Background sentence, in the last sentence of the Introduction, and in the first sentence of the Discussion. Phrases that work: "pilot bioinformatics analysis," "candidate detections," "hypothesis-generating," "warrants confirmatory testing." Phrases to avoid: "established that," "demonstrates the prevalence of," "identifies the cause of."

**Lead with Rickettsiales.** The single most defensible and clinically actionable finding is the alignment-based rescue of Rickettsiales evidence in 11 of 86 AFI cases (~13%), consistent with the well-documented endemic burden of scrub typhus and spotted-fever-group rickettsioses in northeastern Thailand. The Rickettsiales finding fits the diagnostic gap the study set out to address: these are obligate intracellular pathogens that cannot grow on routine blood culture, so a positive bottle / failed-subculture phenotype with 16S detection of Rickettsiales DNA is internally coherent. The one Tier-1 *Orientia* call (`16901195_S5_L001`) is the headline within the headline.

**What NOT to claim** (these were errors in earlier internal drafts and have been corrected):

- Do **not** claim any study sample carries *Burkholderia pseudomallei*. None do, at species level above NTC background. The genus-level *Burkholderia* signal in the cohort is *B. cepacia* complex contamination. The 3 validation-panel cases (where *B. pseudomallei* species reads are 13,744–65,016) demonstrate the assay can detect melioidosis when present — the cohort simply does not contain it.
- Do **not** describe the *Mycoplasmopsis* finding as "strong support for the fastidious-organism hypothesis." Four samples (4.7% of cohort) at high in-sample abundance is a candidate signal, not a cohort-level prevalence claim.
- Do **not** make causal claims linking 16S detections to the bottle signal. The 16S samples are patient blood, not bottle aliquots. Use "candidate," "consistent with," "warrants confirmation."
- Do **not** state that the cohort "lacks anaerobes." *Porphyromonas* and *Desulfovibrio* are retained at low frequency, and V1-V3 primer bias against some anaerobes is a known limitation. "Few anaerobes detected by this assay, with primer-bias caveat" is the honest framing.

**Target journal considerations.** A pilot 16S diagnostic pipeline study with strong validation and a corrected analytical narrative sits comfortably in **Journal of Clinical Microbiology**, **Microbiology Spectrum**, **PLOS Neglected Tropical Diseases** (good fit for the AFI / NE Thailand framing), or **Open Forum Infectious Diseases**. *Lancet Microbe* and *Nature Microbiology* would require a stronger clinical-outcome anchor than the current package provides. A condensed JCM-style draft is already available at [`docs/manuscript/MANUSCRIPT-CONDENSED-DRAFT.md`](../MANUSCRIPT-CONDENSED-DRAFT.md).

## §3. Question prompts for clinicians / EPI team

### 3.1 Per-case follow-up (these are the cases that need the strongest clinical anchoring)

For **each of the 11 Rickettsiales-rescue cases** (see [`AFI-TEAM-HANDOFF.md`](AFI-TEAM-HANDOFF.md) §D.1 for the sample list):

- Was the patient treated with doxycycline (or another tetracycline) empirically? When was it started relative to admission?
- Did the fever defervescence pattern fit a rickettsial-treatment response (typically within 24–48 hours of doxycycline)?
- Was paired acute / convalescent IgM / IgG serology collected for *Orientia tsutsugamushi* and *Rickettsia* spp? If yes, results?
- Was an eschar described on examination? Any maculopapular rash, generalised lymphadenopathy, or hepatosplenomegaly?
- Was there documented exposure consistent with mite or tick contact (rural / agricultural / forested area, fieldwork, recent travel to endemic district)?
- Was Rickettsiales-specific qPCR (or other confirmatory test) performed? If yes, results?
- Final discharge diagnosis and outcome (recovered, transferred, expired)?

For **each of the 4 *Mycoplasmopsis* cases** (samples `13400425_S4_L001`, `15700611_S5_L001`, `10300205_S9_L001`, `09301225_S4_L001`, `24500367_S5_L001` — the appendix table reports 5 detections with read counts 537–6,568, with 4 retained at the V4 filter level):

- Clinical syndrome at presentation — primarily respiratory, primarily systemic, or both?
- Was a Mycoplasma- or Mycoplasmataceae-specific PCR performed? If yes, results?
- What antibiotics were administered? Did the patient respond to macrolide / tetracycline / fluoroquinolone coverage?
- Any underlying conditions that would predispose to Mycoplasma infection (immunosuppression, chronic respiratory disease)?
- Final discharge diagnosis and outcome?

For the **single *Leptospira* candidate** (`09801652_S5_L001`):

- Was paired-serum MAT (microscopic agglutination test) performed? Results?
- Was Leptospira-specific qPCR performed? Results?
- Exposure history: standing water, agricultural work, rodent contact, recent flooding?
- Clinical syndrome: Weil's-disease-like (jaundice + renal failure), pulmonary-haemorrhage form, anicteric leptospirosis?
- Treatment given and outcome?

For the **3 *Brucella* candidates**:

- Was *Brucella* serology (Rose Bengal, SAT, ELISA IgM/IgG) performed? Results?
- Exposure history: unpasteurised dairy, livestock contact, occupational risk (slaughterhouse, veterinary)?
- Clinical features consistent with brucellosis (undulant fever, hepatosplenomegaly, arthritis, prolonged fever > 2 weeks)?
- Treatment given and outcome?

## §4. Skeleton outlines for the sections you own

> *Each bullet is a one-line prompt. Replace the bullet with your prose. No need to keep the bullet structure if it doesn't fit your style.*

### §4.1 Background / Introduction skeleton

- **¶1 — The clinical problem.** Acute febrile illness in NE Thailand has a wide differential. Many of the most common etiologies (Rickettsiales, melioidosis, leptospirosis, brucellosis, Mycoplasma-class) cannot grow or grow poorly on routine aerobic blood culture in the standard 5–7 day window.
- **¶2 — The diagnostic gap.** Positive automated blood culture signal with failed subculture growth is a recognised diagnostic puzzle: the bottle says something was metabolically active, but conventional methods cannot identify it. Empirical therapy proceeds on clinical judgement.
- **¶3 — Why 16S.** 16S rRNA gene sequencing detects bacterial DNA regardless of culturability and provides genus-level (and sometimes species-level) taxonomic assignment. V1-V3 is widely used in clinical 16S workflows; its limitations (anaerobe under-detection, limited intra-Rickettsiales resolution) are well known.
- **¶4 — Aim of this study.** Develop and analytically validate a 16S V1-V3 workflow with Rickettsiales-aware alignment rescue and a species-aware decontamination filter, and apply it as a pilot to NE Thailand AFI cases with positive blood culture but failed subculture recovery. State explicitly that this is a pilot / hypothesis-generating analysis, not a clinical validation or prevalence study.

### §4.2 Methods skeleton — sections the wet-lab + EPI team owns

- **§2.1 Study design and ethical approval.** IRB body, protocol number, enrolment period, hospital(s), inclusion/exclusion criteria. State that 16S samples are patient blood drawn during the same admission, not aliquots of the original positive blood culture bottle.
- **§2.2 Sample populations.** Validation panel composition (drop in from [`AFI-TEAM-HANDOFF.md`](AFI-TEAM-HANDOFF.md) §C if you want a starting point) and study cohort enrolment summary.
- **§2.3 Sample collection.** Blood specimen container, volume, anticoagulant, storage, time-to-extraction.
- **§2.4 DNA extraction.** Kit, manufacturer, version, protocol modifications.
- **§2.5 16S V1-V3 amplification.** Primer sequences (27F / 534R or similar), polymerase, cycling.
- **§2.6 Library preparation and sequencing.** Library prep kit, indexing strategy, MiSeq flow cell + read length + clustering.
- **§2.7 Bioinformatics analysis.** Drop in [`AFI-TEAM-HANDOFF.md`](AFI-TEAM-HANDOFF.md) §B verbatim (or in summary form with a citation to our pipeline source code on GitHub + the back-pocket [`MANUSCRIPT-PACKAGE-FINAL.md`](../MANUSCRIPT-PACKAGE-FINAL.md) §2.4 for detail).
- **§2.8 Concordance definitions and statistical analysis.** Drop in [`AFI-TEAM-HANDOFF.md`](AFI-TEAM-HANDOFF.md) §B concordance box and the line about Wilson 95% confidence intervals.

- **§4.1 Central finding.** Drop in interpretation paragraph E.1 (Rickettsiales as headline).
- **§4.2 Correction on *B. pseudomallei*.** Drop in interpretation paragraph E.2.
- **§4.3 *Mycoplasmopsis* as a candidate fastidious-organism signal.** Drop in interpretation paragraph E.3.

This is where the clinical / case-vignette content goes.
- **§4.6 Diagnostic and treatment implications (EPI team owns).** What this means for empirical antibiotic protocols in NE Thailand AFI workup. What this means for sample-collection practice (paired bottle / blood 16S in future cohorts). What this means for ancillary diagnostic algorithms (when to send Rickettsiales / Mycoplasma / Leptospira PCR).
- **§4.7 Comparison with prior literature (EPI team owns).** Place the Rickettsiales prevalence finding in the context of prior NE Thailand AFI epidemiology studies. Discuss the *B. pseudomallei* absence in the context of regional melioidosis epidemiology.

### §4.4 Limitations skeleton

- **The 4 bioinformatics limitations** — drop in [`AFI-TEAM-HANDOFF.md`](AFI-TEAM-HANDOFF.md) §G verbatim.
- **Pilot sample size** — 86 cases is appropriate for hypothesis generation, not for cohort-level prevalence estimates.
- **Clinical limitations the EPI team should add:** convenience sample, single-region enrolment, no protocolised confirmatory PCR / serology cascade, no prospective treatment-response capture, no anaerobic culture comparator, no paired bottle / blood 16S.
- **6 additional bioinformatics limitations** documented in back-pocket §5 if your target journal expects a comprehensive list.

### §4.5 Conclusion skeleton

- **¶1 — One sentence each on:** what the validated assay achieves, what the pilot cohort found (Rickettsiales as the main finding, candidate fastidious-organism signals in a small minority, no melioidosis), what should be done next (Rickettsiales-specific qPCR confirmation, paired bottle / blood 16S in future cohorts, clinical-outcome linkage).
- **¶2 — Forward-looking close.** This pipeline is suitable for complementary diagnostic use with the caveats stated; the strongest immediate clinical implication is the affirmation of Rickettsiales as a major contributor to positive-culture / failed-subculture AFI in NE Thailand.
